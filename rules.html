Part Two — Who can enter

These rules apply to all four contests equally.

2.1 Free to enter

No purchase or payment of any kind is necessary to enter or to win. There is no entry fee, no buy-in, and no donation is requested at signup. Nothing an entrant buys or donates improves their chance of winning, and no prize pool is funded by entrants.

2.2 Open to

Anyone, at any age, who is a legal resident of the State of Florida.

This is a beta season and entry is limited to Florida. Entries giving an address outside Florida are void, and any prize awarded in error is forfeit. Residency is determined at the time of entry and is not affected by moving afterwards.

The Administrator intends to open further states in later seasons. Each will run under its own rules and its own registration; nothing in these rules applies outside Florida.

Entrants under 18 must supply a parent or guardian's name and email. The entry is not active until that guardian approves it by clicking the link we send them. An under-18 entrant whose guardian does not approve is not entered and cannot win.

2.3 Not eligible

  • Any student on a roster appearing in these brackets. This is absolute and applies whether or not the student's own team is picked.
  • Coaches, assistant coaches and athletic staff of any school appearing in these brackets.
  • Anyone with access to the entry database, and their immediate family.
  • Game officials assigned to any playoff game in these brackets.

2.3 exists so that no person who can affect a result has anything riding on one. Enforce it before the brackets are released, not after.

2.4 One entry each

One entry per person per contest. A person may enter all three. Duplicate entries are reduced to the first confirmed one. Entries submitted by script, bot or any automated means are void.

2.5 Student volunteers and service hours

These contests are run by student volunteers under a registered 501(c)(3). Volunteers are not paid and receive no share of any prize or sponsorship.

Hours are logged as worked and certified by a named officer of the organisation, who issues a letter on the organisation's letterhead stating the dates, the work performed and the total hours. Certification is issued only for hours actually worked and recorded at the time; the Administrator does not certify estimated or retrospective hours.

A volunteer may not enter a contest they administer. Anyone who handles entries, results or standings for a contest — or for a particular Group within the Draft — is ineligible to enter that contest or Group under 2.3. Volunteering elsewhere in the league does not affect eligibility: a volunteer who runs results for one Group may draft in another, and may enter any contest they take no part in administering.

Whether certified hours satisfy a graduation requirement, an honour society, or a scholarship programme is determined by the school or programme concerned and not by the Administrator. Requirements differ by district and some distinguish community service from general volunteering. Volunteers should confirm with their own school before relying on these hours.

2.6 Data

We collect a name, an email, a school, and for under-18 entrants a guardian's name and email. That is all. It is used to run the contests and contact winners, it is never sold or shared with sponsors, and it is deleted [WITHIN 90 DAYS OF THE STATE FINALS]. A guardian may ask us to delete their child's entry at any time by emailing [YOUR EMAIL].

We do not publish full names of entrants under 18. Leaderboards show a first name and last initial unless a guardian tells us otherwise in writing.

Part Three — The Draft

3.1 Groups

The Draft runs as many parallel drafts, not one. Each is held by a Group — a club, school or organisation that recruits its own entrants and brings its own sponsor.

  • A Group is 8 to 16 entrants. Below 8 it does not run; above 16 it splits into two.
  • A Group may draft no more than half the FHSAA playoff field. Sixteen entrants at six teams each uses 96 of roughly 184, which is inside that limit. If the field is smaller in a given season the Administrator reduces the roster size, the Group size, or both, and publishes the figures before any draft is held.
  • Each Group drafts on its own board. Groups do not share a board, so the same school may be drafted in several Groups.
  • Each Group has one sponsor, who funds that Group's prize. Sponsors differ, so Group prizes differ.
  • An entrant belongs to one Group only and may not enter the Draft twice.

Every Group produces a Group Champion. The Group Champions then contest a single Grand Title under 3.6.

At least two Groups must complete a draft for the Grand Title to run. If only one does, that Group's prize is paid as normal and the grand quarter is added to it.

3.2 Group registration closes early, and why

A Group registers by naming its sponsor and the exact amount that sponsor has committed. Registration closes [10 DAYS] before the first kickoff of the regular season, and no Group may join or increase its sponsorship after that.

This deadline is not administrative housekeeping. Prize registration and bonding require the Administrator to state a total prize value before entries open, and until every Group has committed its sponsor that total is unknown. Closing Group registration first fixes the number, so the promotion can be registered and bonded against a real figure rather than a cap.

Once the deadline passes the Administrator publishes, for every Group: the sponsor, the committed amount, the Group prize, and the resulting Grand Pool. Those figures are fixed for the season and are not revised if a sponsor later pays more, less, or nothing.

A sponsor who does not pay does not reduce a prize. The Administrator stands behind every published Group prize and the published Grand Pool, whatever happens between a Group and its sponsor afterwards. Recovering from a defaulting sponsor is the Administrator's problem, never the entrant's.

3.3 The draft

  • Held before the first kickoff of round one, at a time announced by email.
  • Snake order. Draft position is assigned at random and published before the draft begins.
  • Six teams each. Any team on any bracket. Once a team is drafted it is off the board for everyone.
  • An entrant who does not attend is drafted for, in order, by best available seed.

Rosters lock at the first kickoff of round one. No trades, no adds, no drops after that, for any reason, including a team withdrawing or being disqualified.

3.4 Scoring

AchievementPoints
Your team wins a playoff game10
Your team wins a regional title25
Your team wins the state championship50

These accumulate. A state champion in a five-round bracket scores 50 for its five wins, 25 for the regional and 50 for the title — 125 in total.

Every one of these is read directly from the Official Bracket. No individual statistics are used anywhere in this contest.

3.5 Byes, forfeits and cancellations

  • A bye is not a win and scores nothing.
  • A win by forfeit or walkover scores as a win.
  • A game cancelled with no advancing team named scores nothing for either team.
  • A team disqualified after advancing keeps points already scored; the reinstated opponent scores from that round forward.

3.6 The Group Champion

The entrant with the most points in their own Group. Tiebreaks, in order: most state championships, then most total playoff wins, then most teams alive entering the final round, then the earliest confirmed entry. The timestamp guarantees a single Group Champion, so no Group ends in a share.

3.7 The Grand Title, and why raw points cannot decide it

Only Group Champions contest the Grand Title.

Points do not compare across Groups. In an eight-person Group only 48 teams are drafted, so every entrant gets stronger teams and everyone's score runs high. In a sixteen-person Group 96 teams go, the talent is spread twice as thin, and the same performance scores far less. Ranking Group Champions on raw points would hand the Grand Title to the smallest Group almost every year.

So the Grand Title is decided on the Group Index:

Group Index  =  your points  ÷  the average points scored per entrant in your Group

An index of 1.000 means you scored exactly what a typical entrant in your room scored. 2.400 means you scored 2.4 times your room's average, and that means the same thing whether your room held eight people or sixteen. The Grand Title goes to the highest index, carried to three decimal places.

Grand Title tiebreaks, in order: most state championships, then most total playoff wins, then the larger Group — a bigger room is harder to beat — then the earliest confirmed entry.

A smaller Group still carries more luck. The index removes the systematic advantage of a small room but not the wider spread of outcomes in one. A Group of 8 will produce an extreme index more often than a Group of 16 simply through variance. If you want that gone entirely, require every Group to be the same size and score on raw points instead.

3.8 Prizes — how a Group's sponsorship divides

A Group's sponsorship splits in half first, on the same principle as every other contest: half to a school club, half in prizes. The prize half then divides three ways to one.

Share of sponsorshipGoes toOn $1,000
One halfThe club that ran the Group$500
Three quarters of the restThat Group's Champion$375
One quarter of the restThe Grand Pool$125

Every club that runs a Group keeps half of what it raises, whether or not anyone in its room wins anything. A club that runs three Groups keeps half of three sponsorships. This is the fundraiser: the money follows the work of organising, not the luck of the draft.

The Grand Pool is the sum of every Group's quarter and is paid in full to the single Grand Champion, who also keeps their own Group's three quarters. Ten Groups at $1,000 each returns $5,000 to the clubs, pays each Group Champion $375, and builds a $1,250 Grand Pool.

Every one of these figures is fixed and published when Group registration closes under 3.2. Nothing about the Draft prize is estimated once entries open.

Only the prize half is prize value for the purposes of 7.5. The club half is a donation to an organisation and is never paid to an entrant.

Part Four — Tiered Pick 'Em

4.1 Your sheet

Name one projected state champion in every classification, plus one tiebreak number. A sheet missing any classification is not accepted.

Sheets lock at the first kickoff of round one and cannot be changed afterwards for any reason.

4.2 Scoring

Your pick's furthest roundPoints
Wins the state championship100
Loses the state final40
Loses the semifinal15
Eliminated before the semifinal0

Only the furthest round reached counts. A champion scores 100, not 155 — the tiers do not add together.

4.3 Tiebreak

Every sheet carries one number: the combined points scored by both teams in the Class 7A state final. Closest without going over wins, then the earliest confirmed entry. The timestamp guarantees a single winner, so this contest never ends in a share.

With a large field, ties on raw points are the expected outcome rather than an edge case. The tiebreak is what actually decides this contest most years.

4.4 Classification changes

If the Florida High School Athletic Association (FHSAA) merges, splits or cancels a classification after sheets lock, that classification is removed from scoring for every entrant. Nobody scores it and nobody is penalised.

Part Five — Survivor

5.1 How it works

  • Each round, pick one team to win its playoff game.
  • A team you have already picked cannot be picked again, in any later round, for any reason.
  • Your team wins, you advance. Your team loses, you are eliminated.

5.2 The deadline

Picks lock at the first kickoff of the round — not at your own team's kickoff. Everyone is locked at the same instant, so nobody can watch a Thursday result before choosing.

A missed pick is an elimination. There is no auto-pick, no default team and no late entry. A reminder is sent the evening before each deadline, but the deadline applies whether or not you received it.

5.3 Byes, forfeits and cancellations

  • Your team advances by forfeit or walkover: counts as a win, and the team is still burned.
  • Your team has a bye: you may not pick a team on a bye.
  • The game is cancelled with no advancing team named: every entrant who picked that game advances, and the team is not burned.

5.4 Winning

The last entrant standing wins outright.

Survivor is the one contest where a genuine multi-way tie is likely: several entrants can reach the final round together, or the whole field can go out in the same round. Where that happens the outcome is decided in this order:

  1. Most rounds survived. An entrant who reached round four beats one knocked out in round three.
  2. Fewest teams burned to get there.
  3. Earliest confirmed entry.

If every entrant is eliminated in round one, step 1 leaves the whole field level, step 2 separates almost all of it, and step 3 settles the rest. The contest always produces one winner. See 7.8.

Part Six — The National Championship

One ballot, ten schools, and the biggest prize on the site. $20,000 — half to one person, half to two schools.

6.1 Your ballot

Name ten schools, in order, positions 1 through 10 — the final national top ten.

  • Position 1 is your pick for the number one team in the country at the end of the season.
  • Positions 2 through 10 are the next nine, in the order you expect them to finish.

A ballot with fewer than ten schools, or with the same school entered twice, is not accepted. Ballots lock at [KICKOFF OF THE FIRST GAME OF THE REGULAR SEASON] and cannot be changed afterwards for any reason.

6.2 The source, and its limits

This contest is settled against the final national Top 25 published by MaxPreps for the [SEASON] season, as it appears on [SETTLEMENT DATE].

National rankings are editorial opinion, not a result. Unlike a state bracket, no game decides them. They are one publisher's judgement, they are revised during and after the season, and reasonable people disagree with them. By entering this contest you accept that ranking as final and agree not to dispute it — including where it conflicts with a head-to-head result, another publisher's ranking, or your own view of the season.

The Administrator captures and publishes a dated copy of the ranking at the moment of settlement. Revisions published after the settlement date do not reopen or change the outcome, and the Administrator will not restate standings to reflect them.

If MaxPreps does not publish a final national ranking for the season, settlement falls to [NAMED ALTERNATE SOURCE]. If neither publishes, this contest is void and its full $5,000 goes to [YOUR SCHOOL / YOUR CLUB].

MaxPreps is not affiliated with, does not sponsor, and does not endorse this contest. It is named here only as the source whose published ranking the Administrator has chosen to settle against.

6.3 How the winner is found

Settlement runs in steps, and stops at the first step that produces a single entrant.

  1. Position 1. Every entrant who named the actual number one team goes through. If nobody did, every entrant whose position 1 pick finished highest in the ranking goes through instead — so this contest always has a field.
  2. Exact positions. Among those, whoever placed the most of positions 2 through 10 in exactly the right slot.
  3. Right team, wrong slot. Then whoever named the most schools that appear anywhere in the actual top ten.
  4. Closest overall. Then the smallest total displacement — add up how many places each of your ten picks sits away from where it actually finished, counting a school outside the top ten as ten places away. Lowest total wins.
  5. Earliest ballot. If entrants are still level, the earliest confirmed entry wins.

Step 4 exists so that a winner is found on the substance of the ballot rather than on a timestamp. A ballot that is close everywhere beats one that is perfect twice and wrong eight times.

6.4 Worked example

Suppose four hundred entrants all name the same school at position 1, and that school does finish number one. All four hundred clear step 1. At step 2 the field falls to the eleven who placed four of positions 2 through 10 exactly right. At step 3 that falls to two, both of whom named eight of the true top ten. Step 4 separates them on total displacement, and the contest is settled without ever reaching a timestamp.

6.5 Prize — $20,000, split four ways

This contest carries $20,000, the largest on the site, funded by sponsors and divided as follows:

ShareAmountGoes to
One half$10,000The winning entrant, in cash
One quarter$5,000The Champion School — the school ranked number one
One quarter$5,000The Winner's School, if the winner is a student; otherwise the Champion School

Only the $10,000 is a prize. The two quarters are charitable donations made by the sponsor in the winner's name. They are paid directly to the schools, never to the entrant, and the entrant has no claim to either and cannot redirect, decline on the school's behalf, or take them in cash.

6.6 Where the second quarter goes

  • A student wins — of any age, at any school — and the second quarter goes to the school named on their entry.
  • A parent, staff member or community entrant wins, and the second quarter goes to the Champion School instead, which then receives $10,000 in total.
  • A student at the Champion School wins, and both quarters land at the same school, again $10,000 in total.

The Champion School therefore receives a donation every year regardless of who wins, and a student winner adds a second one for their own school.

6.7 If a school cannot accept

Neither school is party to this contest and neither has agreed in advance to receive anything. A school may decline. Some districts and state athletic associations restrict outside donations to a school or its athletic program, particularly where the donation follows from that program's competitive performance — which is exactly what the Champion School's quarter does.

Where a school declines, cannot accept, or does not respond within [60 DAYS] of being contacted, that quarter is donated in equal shares to the school clubs of all entrants in this contest. The winner's $10,000 is never affected by a school's decision.

The Administrator contacts both schools before announcing any donation publicly, and names no school as a recipient until that school has accepted in writing.

6.8 One winner

One winner. No runner-up prizes, no share. The cascade in 6.3 runs to a single entrant and step 5 guarantees it terminates.

Payment of the $10,000 is subject to 7.4: because it far exceeds $600, the winner must supply a completed Form W-9 before any money is released. The school donations do not depend on that form and are paid whether or not the winner completes it; a winner who forfeits under 7.4 loses only their own half.

Prizes are funded entirely by sponsors. Pick 'Em and Survivor carry $5,000 each and split it in half. The Draft is funded per Group and follows 3.8. The National Championship carries $20,000 — $10,000 to one person and $10,000 split between two schools under 6.5:

One winner per contest, with the Draft the single exception: it produces one Champion per Group plus one Grand Champion, all determined under Part Three. In Pick 'Em and Survivor a single entrant takes $2,500 and a further $2,500 goes to the school club named on that entrant's registration. In the National Championship a single entrant takes $10,000, and a further $10,000 is donated to schools under 6.5 and 6.6.

The school half is paid directly to the club or its booster organisation, never to the winner, and the winner has no claim to it. An entrant who names no school, or whose named school declines the funds, forfeits only the school half — their own $2,500 is unaffected.

No entrant pays anything to compete and no prize money comes from entrants. Prizes are not transferable and no substitution is offered. Because entry is free, these contests involve no consideration and are promotions rather than wagers; cash prizes do not change that.

7.2 Prizes for entrants under 18

A prize won by an entrant under 18 is released to the parent or guardian named on the consent record, never directly to the entrant.

Cash is paid to entrants under 18 in the same amount as to any other winner, unless the entrant's school district restricts or prohibits cash awards to students. Where a district does so, that winner receives instead a prize of equivalent value, chosen by the winner from a published list of options — merchandise, gift cards, equipment or experiences.

The value is identical either way. Only the form changes, and only where a district requires it. We confirm the district's position before a prize is released, and a winner is never asked to find that answer themselves.

Ask before you publish. Confirm your district's position on cash awards to students in advance, and list the alternative prize options on the entry page so the choice is visible before anyone enters, not after they win.

7.3 Rostered students

Students appearing on any roster in these brackets cannot enter at all under 2.3, so no prize of any kind — cash or otherwise — reaches a competing athlete. That exclusion, not the form of the prize, is what keeps these contests clear of state association award rules.

7.4 Tax information is required before payment

No taxpayer identification is collected at entry. You do not provide a Social Security number, a taxpayer ID, or an address to play. Nothing beyond a name, an email and a school is requested from anyone who does not win.

Because each prize is $2,500, it exceeds the $600 reporting threshold. So at the point of payment, and only then, each winner must supply a completed IRS Form W-9 — legal name, mailing address and taxpayer identification number — before any money is released. A Form 1099 is issued for the prize.

A prize cannot be paid without this information. A winner who does not return a completed W-9 within [30 DAYS] of being asked forfeits the prize, and both halves — the winner's $2,500 and the school's $2,500 — pass to the next-placed entrant and their school. This is stated here so that no winner is surprised by the request.

For a winner under 18, the Form W-9 is completed by the parent or guardian on the consent record, and the prize and the 1099 are handled through them.

Prize money is the recipient's income. All taxes on it are the recipient's responsibility. The Administrator does not withhold and gives no tax advice.

7.5 Registration and bonding

Prize value across Pick 'Em, Survivor and the National Championship is $15,000 — two $2,500 winner prizes plus the $10,000 paid to the National Championship winner. The Draft adds half of all Group sponsorship, a figure fixed and published when Group registration closes under 3.2, which is why that deadline falls before entries open. The Administrator registers and bonds against the sum of the two. Club and school donations are paid to organisations rather than awarded to entrants and are not prize value. The school halves are paid to organisations rather than awarded to entrants.

Where required, the Administrator registers the promotion and posts a surety bond before entries open. Entry is limited to Florida residents under 2.2, so no other state's registration is required.

7.6 Notification

Winners are notified by email within [14 DAYS] of the state finals. A winner who does not respond within [30 DAYS], or who does not return the tax information required by 7.4, forfeits the prize, which passes to the next-placed entrant.

7.7 Every contest has exactly one winner

None of the four contests can end in a shared or divided prize. Each tiebreak cascade ends in earliest confirmed entry, which is unique to the millisecond, so a single entrant is always identified.

This is deliberate. A cash prize split between an unknown number of people cannot be budgeted, cannot be bonded against, and turns a $2,500 headline into an amount nobody can state in advance. Sponsors are told what a winner receives, and entrants are shown the same figure — so the figure has to be certain.

The timestamp is the last step and never the first. In practice a substantive tiebreak resolves these contests; the timestamp exists so that the rules cannot fail to name someone.

7.8 Sponsorship and the fundraiser

Sponsorship funds $30,000 across Pick 'Em, Survivor and the National Championship — $15,000 to winners and $15,000 to schools — plus whatever each Draft Group's own sponsor puts up. Anything raised beyond that goes to [YOUR SCHOOL / YOUR CLUB]. The full amount raised and the full amount passed to the school are published at the end of the season. Sponsors have no influence over scoring, standings or the selection of winners.

Part Eight — Results and corrections

8.1 Source of results

Results come from the Official Bracket published by the Florida High School Athletic Association (FHSAA). Where our standings and the Official Bracket conflict, the Official Bracket governs.

We do not use, publish or store any individual student's statistics.

8.2 Timing

Standings post the morning after a round completes. There is no live scoring and nothing updates during a game.

8.3 Corrections

We may correct published standings to fix an error in the bracket or in our processing. Corrections may be made up to 72 hours after a round closes, after which that round is final. A correction that changes a prize outcome is published with an explanation.

8.4 Shortened or cancelled seasons

If a round is cancelled it is simply not scored. If the playoffs are abandoned before the semifinals, standings are declared final as at the last completed round, in all three contests.
