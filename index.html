GAME 1

The Draft

Free to enter · Groups of 8–16 · Win your room, then the country

Draft teams, not students. Your club gathers before the playoffs, takes turns picking whole schools off the bracket, and rides them as far as they go. No individual player is drafted, priced or scored anywhere in this league.

A draft caps out around sixteen people — so this game runs as many drafts at once. Every club brings its own sponsor and its own room. Win your room and you're a Group Champion; then every Group Champion in Florida contests one Grand Title.

How the Grand Title works

Points don't compare across rooms. In a room of eight only 48 teams get drafted and everybody's score runs high. In a room of sixteen, 96 teams go and the talent is spread twice as thin.

So the Grand Title runs on the Group Index — your points divided by your room's average. An index of 1.000 is a typical entrant. 2.400 means you scored 2.4× your room, and that means the same thing in a room of eight or sixteen.

Highest index across every Group Champion in Florida takes it.

How the draft runs

  • Snake order. Pick 1 through 12, then 12 back through 1. The last pick of round one gets the first pick of round two.
  • Six teams each. Any school in any of the seven classifications, first come first served. Once a team is taken it is off your Group's board.
  • A Group never drafts more than half the FHSAA playoff field. Sixteen people at six teams each uses 96 of roughly 184 — so the back half of every bracket stays undrafted and late picks still mean something.
  • No trades after the brackets are released. Rosters lock at the first kickoff of round one.

The draft is the event. Run it in a classroom, a cafeteria, or on a call — it takes about forty minutes for twelve people.

Your club brings its own sponsor and keeps half of what it raises, whatever happens in the draft. Run more rooms, raise more.

How your teams score

Your team wins a playoff game10
Your team wins a regional title25
Your team wins the state championship50

Every one of these is readable straight off a published bracket. Nothing here needs a stat line, a box score, or anyone entering yardage by hand.

Why a team scores 85 for a title run, not 50

A champion in a five-round bracket wins five games (50), takes a regional title (25) and the state title (50). Round wins compound, so a deep run in a smaller classification can beat one favourite in the biggest. That's deliberate — it keeps every classification worth drafting.

Group tiebreaks, in order

  • Most state championships on your roster.
  • Most total playoff wins.
  • Most teams still alive entering the final round.
  • Then the earliest confirmed entry, so every room produces one champion.

GAME 2

Tiered Pick 'Em

Free to enter · Unlimited entries · Two minutes to play

One sheet, one shot, everyone at once. A draft caps out around sixteen people. This doesn't. Name a state champion in every classification before round one kicks off, then sit on it for six weeks.

How it scores

Your pick wins the state title100
Your pick loses the final40
Your pick reaches the semifinal15

Only the furthest round counts — a champion scores 100, not 155. Getting one title right is worth more than getting every semifinalist right, which is what makes the sheet worth arguing over.

Where the game is won

Everyone picks the same two or three obvious favourites in the big classifications. Those picks cancel out across the whole field and decide nothing.

The small classifications decide it. They are the ones nobody follows, the ones where a two-loss team can be the best team, and the ones where being right is genuinely worth something.

If you only do your homework in one place, do it at the bottom of the sheet.

The tiebreak, and why it isn't a coin flip

With a hundred entrants and six classifications, ties are not an edge case — they are the likely outcome. So every sheet carries one more question: total combined points scored in the largest classification's state final.

Closest without going over takes it. If that's still level, the earliest verified entry wins.

GAME 3

Survivor

Free to enter · One pick a week · One loss and you're out

Road to the championship. Pick one team to win its playoff game. If it wins you advance. If it loses you are done for the year. And you can never pick that team again.

Round 1 of 5  ·  First round
A typical field loses roughly half its entrants every round. The squeeze is not the losing — it's the burn. By the semifinals your five best teams are all spent, and you are picking from what's left.

The rules, all four of them

  • One team per round. It has to win.
  • A team you have used is gone for good.
  • Your team loses, you are eliminated.
  • Miss the deadline and you are eliminated. There is no auto-pick.

The deadline

Picks lock at the first kickoff of the round, not at your own team's kickoff. Everybody is locked in at the same moment, so nobody gets to watch a Thursday result before choosing.

You'll get a reminder the evening before. Set your own too — forgetting is the most common way people go out.

When everyone goes out at once

It happens. If every remaining entrant is eliminated in the same round, they all share the title and the pool does not roll over.

If somebody survives all the way to the finals, they win outright. Two survivors, split.

Why ten and not one

In most seasons a few hundred people will name the same team at the top, and a lot of them will be right. If position 1 were the whole contest, it would settle on a timestamp.

So positions 2 through 10 are the real game. Everyone who gets the champion right advances, and then the ballot decides it.

How it settles

  • Right at 1? You advance. Nobody right — whoever's pick finished highest advances instead.
  • Most exact slots in 2 through 10.
  • Then most right teams, wrong slot included.
  • Then closest overall — total places away from the truth, across all ten.

VOLUNTEER

Service hours for running it

A registered 501(c)(3) · Documented hours · Certified in writing

Somebody has to run the drafts, read the brackets every Sunday, update the boards and go ask local businesses for sponsorship. Students do all of it — and because this league is a registered 501(c)(3), every hour of that work is documented and certified.

Draft night
3–5hours per Group

Set the room, run the board, keep the snake order straight, handle the entrants who don't show.

Results
2–3hours per round

Read the official brackets, enter every result, post the standings the morning after. Six weekends running.

Sponsorship
10–20hours a season

Build the list, write the ask, walk into local businesses and make the case. The hardest job and the one that funds everything.

Coordination
15–30hours a season

Recruit entrants, chase consent forms, answer questions, keep four contests and every Group moving.

How hours are certified

Volunteers log hours as they go and a named officer of the organisation signs them off. You get a letter on 501(c)(3) letterhead stating the dates, the work and the total — the documentation a school or scholarship programme asks for.

In Florida, Bright Futures requires 100 service hours for Academic Scholars and 75 for Medallion, completed through a nonprofit, government entity or school-sponsored programme. National Honor Society and most graduation requirements work the same way.

Check with your own school before you count on it. Districts differ on what they accept and some distinguish community service from general volunteering. We certify the hours accurately; whether they satisfy a particular requirement is between you and your school.

One rule for volunteers

You can volunteer and still play — but not in a contest you help administer. If you handle entries, results or standings for a Group, you cannot be entered in that Group. Volunteer for the Draft and play Survivor; run the results for one Group and draft in another.

Nobody who can see a result before it is published should have anything riding on it. That rule protects your hours as much as it protects the contest.

How winners get paid

Pick ’Em and Survivor each pay $2,500 to one winner and $2,500 to that winner’s school club. The Draft is funded per Group: half of every Group’s sponsorship goes straight to the club that ran it, and the rest splits ¾ to that Group’s champion and ¼ to a national Grand Pool. The National Championship puts $20,000 in play: $10,000 to one person, $5,000 to the school ranked number one, and $5,000 to the winner’s school if a student wins — otherwise to the number one school as well. Because the prize is over $600, a winner supplies a Form W-9 before we release payment — that is the only point anyone is asked for a tax ID, and it is asked of one person per game. Nobody provides it to enter.

A winner under 18 is paid through the parent or guardian on their consent record, who completes the W-9. If their school district restricts cash awards to students, that winner instead chooses a prize of equivalent value from a published list.

No student on a roster in these brackets can enter at all, so no prize reaches a competing athlete. That exclusion is what keeps this clear of state athletic association award rules — not the form the prize takes.

Who can enter

Open to legal residents of the State of Florida only for this beta season. Entrants under 18 must give a parent or guardian's email, and the entry is not active until that guardian approves it. Students on any roster in these brackets may not enter, nor may their coaches or contest administrators.

How you play

Each round, pick one team to win its playoff game. A team you have already used cannot be picked again. If your team wins you advance; if it loses you are eliminated.

The deadline

Picks lock at the first kickoff of the round, not your own team's kickoff. A missed pick is an elimination. There is no auto-pick and no late entry. A reminder goes out the evening before, but the deadline stands whether or not you received it.

Cancelled and forfeited games

If your team advances by forfeit or walkover, that counts as a win and the team is still burned. If a game is cancelled with no advancing team named, all entrants on that game advance and the team is not burned.

Winning

The last entrant standing wins. If every remaining entrant is eliminated in the same round, they share the title equally. If more than one survives to the finals, they share.

Who can enter

Open to legal residents of the State of Florida only for this beta season. Entrants under 18 must give a parent or guardian's email, and the entry is not active until that guardian approves it. Students on any roster in these brackets may not enter, nor may their coaches or contest administrators.

Your ballot

Ten different schools, in order — the final national top ten. Position 1 is your pick for the number one team in the country; positions 2 through 10 are the next nine in order. Ballots lock at the first kickoff of the regular season and cannot be changed for any reason.

The ranking is an opinion, and you accept it

This contest settles against one published final national ranking, captured on a stated date. National rankings are editorial judgement, not a result — no game decides them, publishers disagree, and they get revised. By entering you accept that ranking as final and agree not to dispute it, including where it conflicts with a head-to-head result or another publisher. Revisions published after the settlement date do not reopen the outcome.

The publisher of that ranking is not affiliated with, does not sponsor and does not endorse this contest. If no final national ranking is published, this contest is void and its full $20,000 is donated to the participating school clubs in equal shares.

How the winner is found

Everyone who names the actual number one advances — or, if nobody does, everyone whose pick finished highest. Then: most exact slots in 2 through 10, then most right teams in any slot, then smallest total displacement across all ten, then earliest ballot. The cascade always ends in one winner; there is no shared prize.

Prize

$20,000 in total. Half — $10,000 — is paid in cash to a single winner. A quarter goes to the school ranked number one in the country. The last quarter goes to the winner's school if the winner is a student, and otherwise to the number one school as well. Only the $10,000 is a prize; the school shares are sponsor donations paid direct to the schools, which the entrant cannot claim, redirect or take in cash. A school may decline — see Part Six. One winner, no runner-up prizes, no share. A winner under 18 is paid through their parent or guardian. A winner must supply a completed Form W-9 before payment; no tax information is collected from anyone else.
