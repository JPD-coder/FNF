GAME 1

Road to State Champs

Free to enter · Starts at districts · $5,000

One team in every classification. Follow them all the way. This is the only game that starts at district play, which is why it is the one running this season. Your six teams score every time they win something — a district, a playoff game, a region, a state title. No elimination, no knockout. You are never out; you are just behind.

How it scores

Your team wins its district10
Your team wins a playoff game15
Your team wins a regional title25
Your team wins the state championship50

These add up. A team that wins its district and runs the table takes 10 + 60 + 25 + 50 = 145 points. Six of those on one sheet is the perfect entry, and nobody has ever had one.

Why there is always a winner

Survivor pools are fun until half the field goes out in week one and the whole thing settles on a coin flip. This one cannot do that.

Everybody's six teams keep scoring for as long as they keep winning, so the board separates on its own. Highest total takes it.

If two people tie: most state titles, then most regional titles, then most district titles, then the earliest confirmed entry. One winner, every year, guaranteed.

Rules — please read
Free to enter

No purchase or payment of any kind is necessary to enter or to win. One entry per person.

Who can enter

Open to students currently enrolled at a Florida public, private or charter high school, who are legal residents of Florida. Parents, staff and community members cannot enter. Entrants under 18 must give a parent or guardian's email, and the entry is not active until that guardian approves it. Students on any football roster in these brackets may not enter, nor may their coaches or contest administrators. Students at an excluded school cannot enter — the list is in rule 2.4.

Your sheet

One FHSAA school in each of the six classifications, all six different. Sheets lock at the first kickoff of district play and cannot be changed afterwards for any reason, including a school withdrawing or being reclassified.

Scoring

10 for a district title, 15 for each playoff game won, 25 for a regional title, 50 for a state championship. These add together. Everything is read directly from the official FHSAA bracket; no individual student's statistics are used anywhere.

One winner

Highest total wins. Ties break on most state titles, then regional titles, then district titles, then earliest confirmed entry. There is no shared prize.

Prize

$5,000: $2,500 to the winner and $2,500 to the winner's school. A winner under 18 is paid through their parent or guardian. Because the prize exceeds $600, a winner must supply a completed Form W-9 before payment; no tax information is collected from anyone else.

Results and independence

Where our standings and the FHSAA's official bracket conflict, the official bracket governs. This contest is not operated, sponsored or endorsed by any school, district, or by the FHSAA.

GAME 2

Class Championships

Free to enter · Two minutes to play · $5,000

One sheet, one shot, everyone at once. Name a state champion in every classification before the playoffs kick off, then sit on it for six weeks. Six picks, no strategy to learn, no board to watch.

How it scores

Your pick wins the state title100
Your pick loses the final40
Your pick reaches the semifinal15

Only the furthest round counts — a champion scores 100, not 155.

The tiebreak decides it

With six classifications and a large field, ties on raw points are the expected outcome, not an edge case.

So every sheet carries one more number: combined points scored by both teams in the Class 6A state final. Closest without going over takes it, then the earliest verified entry.

GAME 3

Undisputed Florida State Champs

Free to enter · One ballot · $20,000

Who is the best team in Florida, regardless of class? Name them, then rank the nine behind them. Class doesn't matter here — a 2A private school and a 6A public are on the same ballot, and arguing about that is the entire point. This is the big one: $20,000, and it pays three ways.

Cash to one winner$10,000
Cash donation to winner's school$5,000
Cash donation to Florida's No. 1 school$5,000Per MaxPreps rankings.

How it settles

  • Right at No. 1? You advance. Nobody right — whoever's pick finished highest advances instead.
  • Most exact slots in 2 through 10.
  • Then most right teams, wrong slot included.
  • Then closest overall — total places away from the truth across all ten.
  • Then earliest ballot. Always one winner.

Settled on the MaxPreps rankings

This game is decided by the final MaxPreps Florida rankings, captured on a stated date and published here at settlement.

A statewide ranking is a published judgement, not a result. No game decides it, it gets revised, and you may think it's wrong. Entering means you accept the MaxPreps rankings as final.

GAME 4

The Draft

Free to enter · Your school, your prize

This one is for people who actually follow Florida high school football. A live snake draft, six schools each, and a roster you cannot change once the playoffs start. If that sounds like work, the other three games take two minutes.

Every Group is exactly sixteen entrants with a sponsor committed for at least $2,500. Each school runs its own draft and settles its own prize — there is no statewide play-off and no overall champion. Win your room and you have won it. Half the sponsorship is yours, half stays with your school.

How the draft runs

  • Sixteen entrants, exactly. A Group with fifteen does not run and cannot enter.
  • A sponsor committed for at least $2,500. Below that the Group does not run either.
  • At least two Groups must register for the Draft to run at all — that is what holds up the published prize floor for the season.
  • Snake order. Draft position is random and published before you start.
  • Six schools each, from any of the six FHSAA classifications. Once a school is taken it is off your Group's board.
  • Florida schools only. Every pick comes off the FHSAA list. Sixteen people at six schools uses 96 of 439, so the board never runs thin.

Your school brings its own sponsor and keeps half of what it raises, whatever happens in the draft. This is the only thing a school is asked to fund — the other three games are on the league.

Why sixteen

It is a practical number, not a competitive one. A live snake draft runs about thirty seconds a pick, so sixteen people over six rounds is roughly forty-five minutes — one sitting, one room, done in an evening.

It also keeps the board deep. Sixteen entrants taking six schools each uses 96 of the 439 in classes 1A-6A, so the last pick of the night is still a real team rather than whatever nobody wanted.

Because Groups never compete against each other, room size carries no advantage either way.

VOLUNTEER

Student Volunteers Welcomed

Somebody has to run the drafts, read the brackets every Sunday, update the boards and go ask local businesses for sponsorship. Students do all of it — and because this league is a registered 501(c)(3), every hour is documented and certified.

Draft night
3–5hours per Group

Set the room, run the board, keep the snake order straight.

Results
2–3hours per round

Read the FHSAA brackets, enter every result, post standings the morning after.

Sponsorship
10–20hours per sponsor

Build the list, write the ask, walk into local businesses. Counts the same whether it funds a statewide prize or your own Draft Group — it is fundraising either way.

Coordination
15–30hours a season

Recruit entrants, chase consent forms, keep four games moving.

How hours are certified

Volunteers log hours as they go and a named officer signs them off. You get a letter on 501(c)(3) letterhead stating the dates, the work and the total.

Florida Bright Futures requires 100 service hours for Academic Scholars and 75 for Medallion, completed through a nonprofit, government entity or school-sponsored programme. Check with your own school before you count on it — districts differ on what they accept.

One rule for volunteers

You can volunteer and still play — but not in a game you help administer. If you handle entries, results or standings for a game, you can't be entered in it. Run results for Road to State Champs and play Class Championships.

Excluded schools

The league reserves the right to exclude any school at its own discretion. Students at an excluded school cannot enter. The school itself stays fully selectable as a team in every game — it is still an FHSAA programme and still plays. Exclusion is not a finding of wrongdoing and no reason is published.
